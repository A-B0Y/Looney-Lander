HAZARD VISION LANDING SYSTEM  -  ARDUINO ROUTE (detection + landing console)
============================================================================
A phone camera looks down at a "Mars yard". The laptop finds ANYTHING that is not smooth, empty ground, outlines each object with its true
shape (round things stay round), and places a round LANDING ZONE in the best empty space and SECURES it at once, however many objects are
on the yard. The Arduino landing console (LEDs, buzzer, servo arrow, distance sensor) lands on it: your hand over the sensor is the lander.

WHAT CHANGED IN THIS VERSION
  - It calibrates ITSELF the first time (and again if the camera is bumped or the lighting changes a lot). Objects that are already on the yard
    are recognised and stay detected, so you never have to babysit calibration. Detected objects do not blink: they appear in about 2 frames
    and only clear after about half a second of calm.
  - The best landing spot is secured immediately (about a quarter of a second), even with many objects on the yard. The ring shrinks to fit a
    tight gap. Only a completely full yard says NO SAFE SITE.
  - The distance sensor is filtered (median of 5 pings, dropout hold, debounce and hysteresis) so a hand over it no longer makes the console
    flip between LOCKED and DESCEND. IMPORTANT: the old sketch also used pin 13 (the sensor's ECHO pin) as a heartbeat LED, which disturbs the
    sensor. That is removed. RE-UPLOAD landing_console.ino.
  - The program survives a lost camera signal and a pulled Arduino cable (it reconnects by itself).

FILES
  landing_console\landing_console.ino   the Arduino program (re-upload it: it changed)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 - WIRE THE ARDUINO (USB unplugged while wiring; every GND to the same rail)
  D6   buzzer + leg (other leg GND)
  D7   green LED  via 330 ohm on the long leg, short leg GND
  D8   yellow LED via 330 ohm on the long leg, short leg GND
  D10  red LED    via 330 ohm on the long leg, short leg GND
  D9   servo signal (orange/yellow); servo red = 5V, brown = GND
  HC-SR04 distance sensor pointing UP:  VCC 5V, GND, TRIG D12, ECHO D13
  D2   not used

PART 2 - PUT THE PROGRAM ON THE ARDUINO
  1. Plug the Arduino into the laptop. Open the Arduino IDE.
  2. File > Open... > landing_console > landing_console.ino
  3. Tools > Board > Arduino AVR Boards > Arduino Uno.   Tools > Port > your port (e.g. COM5).
  4. Click Upload (right arrow). Wait for "Done uploading". The LEDs chase and it beeps = it works.
  5. CLOSE the Serial Monitor if it is open (it blocks the USB port for the laptop).

PART 3 - INSTALL AND TEST
  1. Open settings.bat with Notepad (right-click > Edit). Set COMPORT=... Save and close.
  2. Double-click 1_install_and_check.bat. It must end with "self-test OK".
  3. Double-click 3_test_arduino.bat: the Arduino plays a fake landing: SEARCH (yellow scan blink, radar pings) -> LOCKED (green lock-on strobe)
     -> DESCEND (green/yellow chase, buzzer climbs in pitch) -> TOUCHDOWN (fanfare) -> ABORT (siren, red/yellow strobe, servo shakes).
  4. Double-click 4_test_sensor.bat and wave your hand 5-35 cm above the sensor: alt= numbers appear.
     (No sensor? In the video window press j / k to pretend your hand goes lower / higher, 0 to stop.)

PART 4 - THE CAMERA
  1. Put the phone on a stand looking straight DOWN at the yard (plain surface, evenly lit, no moving shadows, no glare). Do not move it afterwards.
  2. Install Camo on the phone and Camo Studio on the laptop. Same Wi-Fi (or the phone's hotspot for both, or a USB cable).
  3. In Camo Studio: 720p, and LOCK focus, exposure and white balance.
  4. Double-click 2_find_camera.bat. Open camera_0.jpg, camera_1.jpg ... find the one from the phone, put its number in settings.bat (CAMERA=...).
     If it will not open later, change the DSHOW line to:  set DSHOW=--dshow

PART 5 - SET UP THE DETECTOR (optional: it works on its own, these two steps just make it better)
  Double-click 5b_vision_only_no_arduino.bat (or 5_start_demo.bat). The first time, the top bar says AUTO-CALIBRATING for about a second.
  1. YARD (recommended): press  Y,  click the 4 corners of your yard on the picture, press ENTER. Everything outside the yard is ignored
     (table edge, floor, people walking past). Press U to remove it again.
  2. CALIBRATION is automatic. For the very best precision, take everything off the yard and press  C  once. Even if you forget, objects that
     were on the yard stay detected. Press C again after you move the camera or change the lighting a lot (it also does this by itself if the
     picture changes drastically for a few seconds). X forgets the calibration.

PART 6 - READING THE SCREEN
  Red shapes      every object, outlined with its true shape: balls and round things are drawn as exact circles / ovals, boxes and rocks as smooth
                  outlines. There are no boxes drawn over round things.
  Green ring      the LANDING ZONE: the centre of the largest empty circle. Amber and spinning = ACQUIRING, solid green = SECURED (a fraction of a
                  second later, or as soon as the Arduino says LOCKED). Faint dashed circle around it = how much empty space there is. The ring
                  shrinks to fit a tight gap. It turns RED and says BLOCKED the moment anything touches it, and moves to the next best spot.
                  Only a completely full yard says NO SAFE SITE.
  Top bar         lander state (from the Arduino), calibration status, altitude (your hand). Right side: altitude ladder with START and TOUCH lines.
  Bottom bar      object count, how much of the yard is covered, free space around the landing zone, frames per second, current view, SITE quality meter.
  Views (M)       detection / clean video / heat map (what the detector sees: bright = not like the empty yard) / mask.
  Grid (G)        the 16 x 9 cell map the Arduino uses + a servo scale showing which column the arrow points at.
  The landing zone sticks to its spot and only moves when a clearly better place appears. While you are descending it is frozen, so a rock
  dropped on it triggers the ABORT.

PART 7 - RUN THE DEMO
  1. Double-click 5_start_demo.bat (it closes any old copy first). Make sure Yard and Calibration are done (they are remembered).
  2. Put objects on the yard: the red outlines appear within a moment and the landing zone moves to the largest empty space.
  3. Lower your hand over the distance sensor and hold it a moment: below 28 cm = DESCEND (lights chase, buzzer climbs). Below 12 cm the lights
     flash together. Below 7 cm = TOUCHDOWN (fanfare). To cancel a descent lift your hand above about 36 cm for a moment.
  4. ABORT: start a descent, then drop an object onto the green ring. The ring turns red, siren, red/yellow strobe, servo shakes.
  5. Raise your hand above 30 cm for half a second to re-arm after a touchdown or an abort. Q quits.
  6. 6_make_report.bat makes a report page (numbers + chart) of the run.

KEYS
  c calibrate   x forget calibration   y set the yard (click corners, ENTER)   u clear the yard   m view   g grid   space pause
  + / -  sensitivity (+ = fewer hazards, - = more)     j / k  pretend hand lower / higher (0 stop)     h hide text   f full screen   s screenshot   q quit

TUNING TIPS
  Too many red outlines on empty ground     press C on a completely empty yard; check Camo has focus/exposure/white balance LOCKED; remove glare; press +
  A real object is missed                   press - ; press C again; use the heat map view (M) to see what the detector notices; add light
  Objects outside the yard show up          set the yard (Y)
  Landing zone jumps around                 that means the scene is changing: check for moving shadows or a camera that is auto-adjusting
  Slow picture                              the number next to FPS should be 20 or more; close other programs; or use --proc 480x270 in 5_start_demo.bat

DISTANCE SENSOR TIPS (HC-SR04)
  - VCC must go to 5V (not 3.3V), GND to the shared GND, TRIG to D12, ECHO to D13. Pin 13 must have nothing else on it.
  - Point it straight UP with clear space above (no shelves or cables within 50 cm). Use a FLAT hand 10-30 cm above it; fingers pointing at it or
    a hand closer than 3 cm can give bad readings. The console now ignores occasional bad readings, but a very bad setup still hurts.
  - Keep its 4 wires short and firmly seated. A loose wire is the most common cause of a jumpy sensor.
  - Without a sensor you can still test: press j / k in the video window to pretend the hand goes lower / higher, 0 to stop.

TROUBLESHOOTING
  Console flips between LOCKED and DESCEND   re-upload the new landing_console.ino; check the sensor wiring above
  Sits in SEARCH and does not lock           it locks as soon as a spot is offered; if the ring says NO SAFE SITE the yard is completely full
                                             (remove something, or set a smaller yard polygon / check the yard is not covered by the table edge)
  "could not open camera"                  close other apps using the camera, try another CAMERA number, or set DSHOW=--dshow
  "Another Hazard Vision is already running"   close the old video window and its black window (5_start_demo.bat does this for you)
  "Access denied / port busy"               close the Arduino Serial Monitor and any program using the COM port; unplug and replug the USB cable
  Arduino does not react                    check COMPORT in settings.bat matches Tools > Port; re-upload landing_console.ino
